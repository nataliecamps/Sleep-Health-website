Insights

    
          
  • Sleep Duration Patterns: Most individuals sleep 6–8 hours. A smaller group sleeps under 6 hours, suggesting possible sleep deprivation.
    •       
  • Occupation and Sleep Quality: Active professions show higher sleep quality, while sedentary roles trend lower.
    •       
  • Physical Activity & Sleep Duration: Increased activity levels generally correlate with longer sleep durations.
    •       
  • Boxplot Observations: Those with over 30 minutes of daily physical activity exhibit more consistent and higher sleep quality.
    •       
  • Correlation Heatmap: Physical activity is moderately positively correlated with sleep metrics, while stress shows a strong negative correlation with sleep quality.
    •     
  
  
    

Conclusion

    

      Hypothesis: “Individuals who engage in regular physical activity have better sleep quality and duration compared to those with sedentary lifestyles.”     

    

      The visualizations and data analysis support this hypothesis:     

    
          
  • Physical activity positively correlates with sleep quality and duration.
    •       
  • Active individuals consistently experience better sleep outcomes, regardless of occupation.
    •       
  • Stress negatively impacts sleep and should be addressed alongside physical activity.
    •     
    

Recommendations:

    
          
  • Promote at least 30 minutes of daily physical activity for improved sleep.
    •       
  • Implement stress management strategies to further support sleep health.
    •       
  • Encourage movement breaks or standing desks in sedentary work environments.
    •     
    

Consistent physical activity is not just beneficial for physical health—it’s a strong predictor of quality sleep.
